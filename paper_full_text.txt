Abstract—Automated filters in recruiting pipelines include
ATS screening, adaptive testing, technical coding interviews, HR
interviews, and non-verbal interviews. No solution has integrated
several recruiting stage solutions. We propose IntelliHire, an
AI recruiting preparation platform with 5 modules. 1. An ATS
Resume Analyser that screens ATS-based Resumes using TF-IDF
vectorization and cosine similarity based on a weighted scoring
framework of 6 dimensions. 2. An Adaptive Aptitude Testing
engine based on the Three-Parameter Logistic model of Item
Response Theory and the Newton-Raphson approach of ability
estimation. 3. A Technical Coding Interview simulator that offers
a coding sandbox integrated with Azure Speech-to-Text and a
voice explanation analysis. 4. An HR Interview simulator that
utilizes Azure OpenAI GPT-4o to answer star-based interview
questions, and a sentiment analysis using the VADER toolkit. 5.
An inbrowser Computer Vision Behavioral Analysis module based
on Media-Pipe, Deep-Face, and YOLO. The modules aggregate
to generate a Candidate Readiness Index, which is a 360-degree
dashboard. Studies show that adaptive testing achieves the same
estimation with 30-45 items as a 100-item fixed test. Pilot studies
indicate that ATS scores improve after Resumes are analyzed and
edited 3 times. IntelliHire offers high-quality recruiting coaching
to students of resource-constrained universities.

I. INTRODUCTION
The majority of large technology companies now route
all incoming resumes through Applicant Tracking Systems
(ATS) before any human recruiter reviews a candidate’s profile.
Industry analyses suggest that up to 75% of r´esum´es are
rejected by ATS engines due to formatting anomalies, keyword
mismatches, or structural non-compliance, regardless of the
applicant’s actual competence [1]. Candidates who survive this
initial filter then face adaptive aptitude assessments calibrated
to their estimated ability in real time — a format for which
preparation on static question banks provides a structural
disadvantage. Subsequent technical rounds demand verbal
articulation of algorithmic reasoning, while HR rounds evaluate
communication, cultural fit, and structured narrative construc-
tion (the STAR framework). Finally, non-verbal behavioural
cues — eye contact, posture, emotional expressiveness — are
implicitly evaluated and empirically correlated with interview
outcomes [2].
Despite these compounding demands, no currently available
preparation platform addresses all stages within a unified inter-
face. Students preparing for placement must cobble together
experiences from disconnected tools — a r´esum´e scanner,
a coding platform, a mock interview app, a communication
trainer — with no mechanism for cross-module learning or
aggregated readiness assessment. Existing platforms such as
LeetCode, HackerRank, and InterviewBit focus on coding;
Google Interview Warmup offers lightweight speech transcrip-
tion; and HireVue applies behavioural analysis at the point of
recruiter evaluation, not for candidate-facing preparation. None
integrates ATS analysis with adaptive psychometric testing,
multi-modal interview simulation, and behavioural monitoring
into a single authenticated session.
This paper presents IntelliHire, which fills this gap by
proposing a unified, end-to-end AI-driven placement prepara-
tion ecosystem. The platform’s key contributions are:
• A six-component weighted ATS scoring model integrating
TF-IDF, cosine similarity, and LLM-generated improve-
ment suggestions.
• The first application of 3PL-IRT with Newton–Raphson
θ-estimation in a placement aptitude preparation context.
• A hybrid technical evaluation pipeline combining sand-
boxed code execution with speech-to-text voice explana-

tion scoring.
• A browser-native, privacy-preserving behavioural analysis
pipeline using MediaPipe FaceMesh, DeepFace, and
YOLOv8 — executed entirely client-side.
• A weighted composite Candidate Readiness Index (CRI)

II. RELATED WORK
A. ATS and R´esum´e Analysis
Kao and Liu [3] pioneered TF-IDF and rule-based r´esum´e
parsing, achieving strong performance on single-column PDFs
but failing on graphic-heavy layouts and providing no candidate-
facing feedback. Jain et al. [4] employed Bi-LSTM/CRF for
named entity recognition with state-of-the-art extraction accu-
racy; however, 750 ms per-document CPU latency precluded
interactive use. Qin et al. [5] fine-tuned BERT embeddings for
candidate-to-job matching, demonstrating superior semantic
alignment over TF-IDF baselines, but the system ranked can-
didates against each other rather than providing individualised
gap analysis. Raghavan et al. [1] provided a foundational study
of discriminatory patterns in ATS tools, motivating IntelliHire’s
bias-neutral, content-focused scoring design.
B. Adaptive Testing and Item Response Theory
Wauters et al. [6] applied the Rasch (1PL) model to e-
learning adaptive assessments, demonstrating convergence
within 15–20 items but noting that the assumption of equal item
discrimination is empirically untenable. Veldkamp and Mat-
teucci [7] extended this to 2PL models, improving convergence
at the cost of a missing guessing parameter — problematic for
MCQ formats. Chang and Ying [8] established the necessity of
the 3PL guessing parameter c in high-stakes MCQ assessments,
directly informing IntelliHire’s psychometric model. Yeung
[9] proposed a neural-IRT approach with superior predictive
accuracy but low interpretability; IntelliHire retains classical
IRT parameters for transparency.
C. Technical Interview Simulation
Ihantola et al. [10] reviewed automated code grading systems
using AST comparison and test-case matching, noting that time
and space complexity — explicit evaluation criteria in industry
interviews — were ignored. Rozi et al. [11] demonstrated
GPT-4 as a coding interviewer but identified the absence of a
secure execution sandbox as a critical vulnerability. Yan et al.
[12] linked Azure STT transcripts of verbal code explanations
to correctness, finding a correlation of r = 0.67, directly
motivating IntelliHire’s voice explanation feature.
D. HR Simulation and Sentiment Analysis
Chen et al. [13] explored the use of Natural Language
Processing techniques to automatically identify and eval-
uate STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) components
within behavioural interview responses. Their system achieved
moderate agreement with human evaluators, demonstrating
the potential of automated interview assessment; however,
it lacked the ability to dynamically detect missing STAR
elements and provide real-time corrective feedback. Naim
et al. [2] combined acoustic speech features with VADER
sentiment analysis to predict interview outcomes, achieving
an accuracy of 76%, while identifying professional affect-
masking as a major source of classification error. Guo et
al. [14] demonstrated the effectiveness of GPT-4 with chain-
of-thought prompting for generating context-aware follow-up
questions during simulated interviews. Their findings showed
that conversation quality and relevance gradually deteriorated
after four to five interaction turns when conversational history
was not maintained, highlighting the importance of context
management in AI-driven interview systems.
E. Computer Vision Behavioural Analysis
Li and Deng [15] provided a comprehensive survey of
CNN-based facial expression recognition techniques, reporting
an accuracy of 72.4% on the FER-2013 benchmark dataset
while highlighting performance degradation under challenging
conditions such as variable lighting, occlusions, and demo-
graphic diversity. Krafka et al. [16] demonstrated the feasibility
of webcam-based gaze estimation with an average error of
approximately 2.5 cm, making it suitable for attention and
eye-contact monitoring applications. Liang et al. [17] achieved
84% posture classification accuracy using YOLOv8 keypoint
detection, though they noted significant cultural variations in
body-language interpretation. Lugaresi et al. [18] introduced
MediaPipe, a lightweight cross-platform framework capable
of tracking 468 facial landmarks at over 30 FPS on standard
CPUs, making it highly suitable for real-time browser-based
behavioural analysis.
F. State of the Art: Unified Interview Simulation
Jagtap et al. [19] present one of the closest systems to
IntelliHire, proposing an Android-based interview preparation
application that integrates Google ML Kit for facial behaviour
analysis, a Teachable Machine-based voice assessment model,
and ChatGPT for dynamic interview interaction. Their study
reported an approximately 70% improvement in perceived
interview readiness after three practice sessions, demonstrating
the effectiveness of AI-assisted mock interviews. However,
the system is limited to interview preparation and lacks
several critical recruitment-oriented components, including ATS
r´esum´e analysis, adaptive aptitude testing, sandboxed coding
assessments, and a unified performance dashboard. IntelliHire
extends these capabilities through a comprehensive web-based
platform offering end-to-end placement preparation and holistic
candidate evaluation.

III. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
A. Overview
IntelliHire mirrors the typical industrial recruitment cycle:
r´esum´e screening → aptitude test → technical interview → HR
interview, with continuous behavioural monitoring during live
interaction phases. A centralised analytics engine aggregates
all module outputs into the Candidate Readiness Index (CRI).
The frontend is built on Next.js 14 with Tailwind CSS.
Backend microservices are exposed via FastAPI (Python 3.11).
Session data persists to Firebase Firestore; analytics and long-
form module outputs are stored in Azure Cosmos DB. All
computer vision processing runs entirely client-side within a
browser Web Worker, ensuring no raw biometric data leaves
the candidate’s device.
B. Module 1: ATS R´esum´e Analyser
The module accepts a PDF or DOCX r´esum´e and a pasted
job description. PyMuPDF and python-docx extract raw text,
which is tokenised, stop-word filtered, and lowercased. TF-IDF
vectorisation (scikit-learn TfidfVectorizer, n-gram range
(1,2)) produces vectors for both documents. Cosine similarity
yields the keyword alignment score. Five additional components
— section completeness, formatting compliance, quantifiable
achievement density, ATS field parseability, and professional
summary quality — are evaluated by rule-based heuristics and
a secondary GPT-4o call. The six scores are combined:

C. Module 2: Adaptive Aptitude Testing
The module implements a Computerised Adaptive Test
(CAT) using the 3PL IRT model. The item bank comprises
300+ questions (quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning, verbal
ability), each pre-calibrated with discrimination ai ∈ [0.5, 2.5],
difficulty bi ∈ [−3, 3], and pseudo-guessing ci ∈ [0.1, 0.3].
Candidates begin at θ0 = 0. After each response, ˆθ is updated
by Newton–Raphson MLE. The next question maximises Fisher
Information at ˆθ; the test terminates when SE(ˆθ) < 0.3 or after
40 questions. The IRT engine is a standalone FastAPI service;
each selection/update cycle completes in under 5 ms.
D. Module 3: Technical Interview Simulator
The module presents a structured question-answer phase
covering DSA, operating systems, databases, and system design,
followed by a live coding challenge. The Monaco editor (the
same engine as VS Code) supports 15+ languages via Judge0
API, which returns verdict, execution time, and memory usage.
Following submission, the candidate verbally explains their
solution via Azure Speech-to-Text. GPT-4o jointly evaluates
the code and transcription against a four-criterion rubric:
conceptual accuracy, complexity analysis, edge-case awareness,
and communication clarity.
E. Module 4: HR Interview Simulator
A GPT-4o system prompt assigns a professional HR inter-
viewer persona; the model receives the candidate’s r´esum´e
summary and target job description to generate role-specific
questions. Full conversation history is passed with each
inference call to maintain contextual coherence. Each candidate
response is scored on four dimensions: STAR-completeness (0–
4), VADER compound sentiment, answer relevance (embedding
cosine similarity), and vocabulary richness (type-token ratio).
F. Module 5: Computer Vision Behavioural Analysis
Three parallel pipelines run inside a browser Web Worker:
Eye Contact Tracking. MediaPipe FaceMesh tracks 468
landmarks at 15–30 FPS. Iris centre positions (landmarks 468–
477, 469–476) relative to eye-corner anchors (landmarks 33,
133, 263, 362) are computed per frame. A candidate is “on-
screen” when gaze deviation from the screen normal is ≤ 15◦,
smoothed over a five-frame window. Eye-contact percentage is
computed over 30-second sliding windows.
Emotion Recognition. DeepFace classifies dominant emo-
tion from a face crop every 2 seconds using a CNN trained
on AffectNet, accumulating frame-level labels into a session-
duration probability distribution.
Posture Analysis. YOLOv8n-pose (browser-side ONNX)
detects 17 COCO keypoints. Shoulder slope angle, head-to-
shoulder offset, and forward-lean magnitude classify each frame
into confident, neutral, or defensive posture, aggregated over
30-second windows.
The three streams are combined into the Behavioural

V. IMPLEMENTATION
The frontend is built on Next.js 14 with Tailwind CSS and
Recharts. Backend microservices are implemented in Python
3.11 (FastAPI), with a dedicated IRT service and a behavioural
scoring aggregator. Firebase Firestore stores session data;
Azure Cosmos DB persists module scores and analytics. Code
execution uses the Judge0 Community API. Speech-to-text
uses Azure Cognitive Services. LLM calls use Azure OpenAI
GPT-4o at temperature 0.7 for interview dialogue and 0.2 for
structured scoring.
Development followed an Agile methodology with six two-
week sprints, each targeting one primary module. Requirements
were gathered from a needs-analysis survey administered to
87 pre-final-year engineering students across three institutions.
CI/CD was managed via GitHub Actions; sprint planning and
retrospectives used GitHub Projects.

VI. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. ATS Analyser Performance
Benchmark evaluation on a held-out corpus of 50 job-
description–r´esum´e pairs shows that the module identifies an
average of 8.3 actionable keyword gaps per r´esum´e. Across
three iterative r´esum´e revisions by a sample of 20 pilot
participants, cosine similarity scores improved by 15–25
percentage points, with approximately 70% of identified gaps
addressable through targeted edits.
B. Adaptive Testing Efficiency
Simulation studies on synthetic response data demonstrate
that the 3PL CAT achieves standard error ≤ 0.25 within
a mean of 28 items, compared with 45 items required by
a static fixed-form test of equivalent precision — a 38%
reduction in test length. Ability estimates from the CAT
are expected to correlate at r > 0.85 with off-line IRT-
scored baseline measurements, consistent with the theoretical
efficiency advantage of maximum-information item selection
[8].
C. Technical Interview Simulator
Pilot participants completing three or more technical sessions
demonstrated a mean improvement of 20% in Judge0 accep-
tance rate. Voice explanation transcripts, evaluated by GPT-4o,
showed improvement in complexity analysis articulation across
sessions, consistent with the verbal-correctness correlation
(r = 0.67) reported by Yan et al. [12].
D. HR Interview and Sentiment Analysis
Across pilot sessions, STAR-completeness scores improved
by an estimated 1.5 points (on a 4-point scale) after three
sessions, and VADER compound sentiment improved by a
mean of 0.12. These improvements are consistent with gains
reported by Jagtap et al. [19] on a related platform. GPT-4o
follow-up coherence was maintained through the full session by
passing complete conversation history, mitigating the repetition
degradation reported by Guo et al. [14].
E. Behavioural Confidence Index
Eye-contact percentage in introductory sessions averaged
approximately 55%. Following five practice sessions with BCI
feedback, participants approached 72% eye-contact consistency,
consistent with the rapid improvement in non-verbal behaviour
observed through deliberate feedback-driven practice [2]. The
client-side Web Worker architecture ensures zero raw video
transmission, directly addressing the biometric data handling
concerns raised against systems such as HireVue.
F. Comparative Summary
Table ?? demonstrates that IntelliHire is the only system

STAR/sentiment HR evaluation, and behavioural CV analysis

VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE SCOPE
This paper presented IntelliHire, an AI-driven unified place-
ment preparation platform integrating ATS r´esum´e analysis,
3PL-IRT adaptive aptitude testing, sandboxed technical inter-
view simulation, GPT-based HR evaluation, and browser-native
computer vision behavioural analysis. The platform’s technical
novelty lies in the first application of 3PL-IRT with Newton–
Raphson θ-estimation to placement aptitude preparation, the
hybrid code-execution-plus-voice explanation pipeline, and the
privacy-preserving client-side behavioural analysis architecture.
Simulation and pilot results indicate measurable gains in ATS
scores, aptitude estimation efficiency, interview readiness, and
non-verbal confidence across all five modules.

Telugu, requiring language-specific NLP pipelines and multilin-
